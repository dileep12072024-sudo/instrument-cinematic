IST: Department of Instrument Technology, Andhra University

The Department of Instrument Technology (IST) is part of AU College of Engineering (A), Andhra University, Visakhapatnam. The department began as an instrument manufacturing section serving the university, and has grown into a centre for teaching and research in instrumentation and control engineering. The boards in this hall record those who have led it.

Andhra University is accredited A++ by NAAC (CGPA 3.74, valid to 2030) and ranked 23rd among Indian universities in NIRF 2025.

History

1926
Andhra University is founded in Visakhapatnam.
1937
An Instrument Manufacturing Section is established.
1946
The section joins the Department of Applied Physics, covering instrumentation, optics and electronics.
1966
Research begins in thin-film technology, photo-elasticity and ultrasonics.
1990
It moves to the College of Engineering as the Department of Instrument Technology.
2001
The M.Tech programme in Instrumentation and Control is introduced.
2008
The west wing is inaugurated on 25 June by Prof. Y. C. Simhadri, Vice-Chancellor.
2011
The renovated department is inaugurated on 8 May by Prof. Beela Satyanarayana, Vice-Chancellor.
2015
The first floor of the east wing is inaugurated on 30 August.

Programmes and admissions

The department offers undergraduate, postgraduate and doctoral programmes. Admission enquiries are handled by the department office on the west corridor.

B.Tech, Instrumentation Engineering

4 years. Admission through AP EAPCET, with 45% in physics, chemistry and mathematics (40% for reserved categories).

M.Tech, Instrumentation and Control

2 years. GATE-qualified candidates are admitted first; remaining seats are filled through AP PGECET.

Ph.D.

Research. Through the university’s research admissions, in sensors, MEMS, nanotechnology, and instrumentation and automation.

  • To develop the engineering breadth to analyse problems, innovate, and design products that serve society.

  • To encourage entrepreneurship and innovation in meeting social, technical and business challenges.

Laboratories and research

The department maintains eleven laboratories: six in the west wing, the Simulation, Electronics II and Measurements labs on the east side of the ground floor, and the Device Fab and Biomedical Instrumentation labs on the first floor of the east wing.

Industrial Instruments Lab

Practical training in the instruments of the process industry. Transmitters and transducers for pressure, flow, level and temperature are studied, tested and calibrated against reference standards.

Process Control Lab

Closed-loop control of level, flow, pressure and temperature. Students tune industrial controllers and evaluate their performance by the quarter-decay ratio and the IAE, ISE and ITAE criteria.

DCS Lab

The final-year laboratory for distributed control systems and programmable logic controllers, the automation architecture of refineries, power stations and process plants.

Sensors and Transducers Lab

The characterisation and calibration of transducers for strain, displacement, temperature, pressure and light, from the physical quantity to a measurable electrical signal.

Control Systems Laboratory

Experiments in the time and frequency response of physical systems, stability analysis, and the design of compensators and controllers.

Machines Lab

Testing and performance analysis of DC machines, transformers and induction motors, the electrical machines that drive industrial plant.

Simulation Lab

Modelling and simulation of instruments, processes and control loops, so that designs can be analysed and validated before they are built.

Electronics Lab II

Analogue and digital electronic circuits, from amplifiers and oscillators to logic, the building blocks inside every instrument.

Measurements Lab

Electrical and electronic measurement with bridges, meters and oscilloscopes, with emphasis on accuracy, calibration and the analysis of measurement error.

Device Fab Lab

Fabrication and testing of sensors and microdevices, supporting the department’s research in sensors, MEMS and nanotechnology.

Biomedical Instrumentation Lab

Instrumentation for physiological measurement: the acquisition of cardiac, muscular and neural signals (ECG, EMG and EEG) and the principles of patient safety.

Research areas

  • Sensors and sensor networks
  • MEMS
  • Nanotechnology
  • Instrumentation and automation
  • Thin films, photo-elasticity and ultrasonics
  • Research projects funded by UGC, DST, AICTE and DRDO, with support under the UGC Special Assistance Programme and DST-FIST.
  • Approximately fifty research publications each year in IEEE, Springer and Elsevier journals.
  • Testing and calibration of industrial instruments for VSP, L&T, Reliance and Ramky.
